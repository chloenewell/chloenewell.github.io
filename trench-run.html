Trench Run

A 20-Minute Gradual Release Reading Activity
Grade 4 · "When an Island Explodes" · Portfolio Design Sample — Prepared by Chloé Newell
[System Overview]

The Whole System, at a Glance

A 20-minute independent Grade 4 digital activity mapping directly to CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.4.3 (Cause & Effect / Relationships Between Events), using a gradual-release arc — I DO, WE DO, YOU DO — with three short mini-games that turn each cause-effect link the student traces into another node lit up on the Trench grid.

High Concept: a guided third-person 3D adventure where the student's Runner avatar drops into a cyberpunk data-world alongside Cipher, a scripted teen tech-mentor NPC. Pedagogical progression maps directly to a chain of "Data Gates" — tracing cause and effect is the structural hero of a continuous world.

Design Assumptions

  • Pre-Established Premise: the reward premise ("stronger effort earns more mini-game time") is established before this lesson and not re-explained mid-session.
  • Reading Passage: "When an Island Explodes" is based on the real 1963 formation of Surtsey, Iceland — chosen because its chain of causes (pressure → rupture → lava → landform → colonization) is concrete, sequential, and age-appropriate.
  • Text Complexity: intentionally above grade-level sentence complexity. Text-to-speech is available platform-wide, so this activity tests comprehension, not decoding.

Adaptive Progression Logic (System-Level)

Mastery of RI.4.3 isn't proven via one session — this module is a node in an adaptive sequence, powered by the detour/checkpoint telemetry already built into WE DO and YOU DO. No separate tracking system required.

  • Clean Run (zero detours): mastery flagged; a short bonus "side quest" re-tests the same skill in a new context later, so a fast run gets rewarded, not just trusted.
  • Some Support Needed (1–2 detours): held on the current skill; next session serves a same-skill variant before advancing.
  • Heavy Support Needed: routed to the specific prerequisite skill that broke down, then loops back once it clears.

Reading Passage — When an Island Explodes

Paragraph 1: In 1963, pressure from magma deep beneath the ocean floor near Iceland finally became too strong to hold back. The seafloor cracked open, and lava poured into the freezing ocean water above it. Because the lava cooled and hardened almost instantly on contact with the cold sea, layer after layer piled up in the same spot. Within days, a brand-new island called Surtsey had risen out of the water where there had been nothing but open ocean before.

Paragraph 2: At first, Surtsey was nothing but bare black rock, too harsh for anything to survive. But because wind and ocean currents kept carrying in seeds, insects, and driftwood, tiny signs of life slowly began to appear. Birds were drawn to the rocky cliffs, and their droppings added nutrients to the ground. As a result, thin patches of soil formed in places that had been solid rock only years before. Scientists are still tracking exactly how fast a totally lifeless island can transform into one with real plants, insects, and nesting birds.

Paragraph 3: Surtsey didn't stay the same size for long. Because ocean waves constantly crash against its soft volcanic rock, large sections of the island have slowly worn away since it first formed. As a result, Surtsey is now less than half the size it was in the 1960s. Scientists protect the island by banning visitors, so that the plants and animals that arrive keep arriving naturally, without any human footprints changing the pattern.

Think-Aloud: Tracing the Chain

[SEE] Key phrases glow as Cipher names them: "pressure," "cracked open," "because the lava cooled."
Cipher:

"First pass: magma's pushin' up, pressure buildin' for who knows how long. That's cause number one. Then — crack — the seafloor busts open. That's the effect of the pressure, but it's ALSO the cause of what happens next: lava floods into the cold water."

Cipher:

"Here's the key move, right in the middle: 'Because the lava cooled and hardened almost instantly.' See that word 'because'? That's the log handin' me the wiring diagram for free. Cold water touches lava → lava hardens fast → layers stack up → island rises. One chain, four links."

Cipher:

"That's the move every time: find the trigger, follow the 'because,' trace where each effect turns into the next cause."

Quick Check

Cipher: "Which chain shows what actually happened, in order?"

  • A) Island formed → lava cooled → magma pushed up
  • B) Magma pressure built → seafloor cracked → lava hit cold water → island rose ✓
  • C) Lava cooled → seafloor cracked → magma pressure built
Branching logic

If B: green check lights the chain; Cipher affirms and moves on. If A/C: soft glow, re-highlight, one retry. Second miss narrows to two choices and Cipher supplies the answer — no third attempt, to protect the time budget.

🎮 Mini-Game 1 — Chain Reaction Run · 1:00

Purpose: Solidify what was just modeled — reinforces paragraph 1's cause chain through the mechanic itself.

Quick take: 3-lane dodge-and-swipe, Subway Surfers-style — but the Runner races just ahead of a spreading lava front, and every dodge causes a chunk of ground to harden into track beneath the Runner's feet. By the run's end, the assembled track has become the new island.

Specs: Lane-switch + jump controls · 4 evenly spaced hardening milestones mark visible island growth · brief freeze beat at each milestone pulls the camera back to show growth · win condition: reach the far platform / "SIGNAL LOCKED"; failure is soft.

Reward: Baseline 1 life. First-try I DO correct → +2 (3 total); needed a retry/hint → +1 (2 total). Hard cap at 3.

🎮 Mini-Game 2 — Driftline Chase · 1:00

Purpose: Solidify paragraph 2's first link — wind and currents carrying seeds, insects, and driftwood onto bare rock — through the mechanic itself.

Quick take: A steering-and-catch mini-game — the Runner banks along a winding current-line to intercept drifting seeds and driftwood. Each catch deposits a fleck of green onto a tiny island silhouette. Steering toward a natural force to catch what it carries is a literal enactment of "currents carried in seeds."

Specs: Steer left/right along a winding path, ~15–20 sec · drift items cross at intervals · steering into an item auto-catches it, no separate tap · missed items don't end the run · win condition: island silhouette reaches full green coverage.

Reward: Baseline 1 life. No detours across WE DO's three checkpoints → +2 (3 total); one or more detours → +1 (2 total). Hard cap at 3.

🎮 Mini-Game 3 — Grid Overload · 1:00

Purpose: Culminating "you cracked the chain" payoff, and a final embodied rep of the full cause-effect chain right as the summary sentence settles in.

Quick take: Flappy Bird-style tap-to-flap through gaps — but each of the 2–3 legs has its own terrain: bare rock → soil patches → green life. The terrain completes the chain as the Runner completes the flight; the hand-off beat between legs is visual proof one link fired the next.

Specs: Tap to boost/ascend, gravity pulls down between taps · 2–3 legs, obstacles per leg · each leg swaps to the next terrain stage using one reusable art set · win condition: reach the Mainframe core with full green terrain, "SIGNAL LOCKED" landing.

Reward: Baseline 1 life for everyone, including heavy YOU DO scaffolding. Bonus scales with support needed: no detours → 3 lives; one detour → 2; multiple → 1 (baseline only).

Vertical Slice / Style Guide

Technical & World Constraints

  • Engine Environment: one uninterrupted 3D map across five linear zones (Spawn Bay → Grid Runway → Signal Tower → Server Vault → Mainframe). No hub or loading screens.
  • Camera: third-person follow cam during free-roam; locks to a fixed framing at the start of each mini-game; smooth transition cut, never a hard cut.
  • Input — Explorer/Free-Roam: standard third-person movement with a single context-sensitive interact button.
  • Input — Mini-Game 1: 3-lane lane-switch + jump; dodges double as the build input.
  • Input — Mini-Game 2: steering (left/right bank) to intercept drifting items; contact is the catch.
  • Input — Mini-Game 3: tap-to-ascend / release-to-fall, gravity-based.
  • Input — Fail-Safe Ladder: four reusable input types shared across every checkpoint in every future module — tap, drag-to-slot, drag-to-reorder, tap/type.
  • State Machines: checkpoint boundaries trigger an absolute gameplay freeze. Auto-resume disabled; ladder rungs are sub-states sharing this same freeze/resume behavior.
  • Accessibility: text-to-speech available platform-wide. Correct/incorrect states signaled by icon + color together, never color alone.

Character & Asset Specifications

  • Cipher: single, immutable 3D character model used uniformly across all states — tech-visor teen mentor, neon-trim streetwear, holographic wing pack.
  • Runner Avatar: modular customization across three slots — head (headset with glowing mic boom), torso (tee/jacket with pixel-circuit patch), back accessory (Holographic Glider, unequipped until launch). One rig, swappable slot assets.
  • Spatial Blocking: Cipher stands beside the Runner in I DO, paces alongside in WE DO, steps physically away in YOU DO.
  • Sprite Economy: chain-link console runs on one core sprite sheet with badge overlays ("Link 1," "Link 2") — no asset re-draws.
  • Fail-Safe Ladder UI Kit: word-bank chips, scrambled-packet tiles, fill-in-the-blank line — one reusable asset set across every checkpoint in every future module.
  • Mini-Game Asset Reuse: track-hardening tiles, drift-catch sprites, and terrain-stage backgrounds are each a single reusable set — the embodiment swaps per lesson, the asset system doesn't.

UI, Audio, & Script Tags

  • UI & Audio: map-centric, minimal chrome. Continuous ambient synth-hum audio bed under the whole session.
  • Chime Language: one "correct" chime reused everywhere; one "gentle miss" tone; one distinct "ladder rung advance" chime — audio signals depth of support without extra dialogue.
  • System Scripting Tags: [SEE] / [HEAR] (environment/audio), [PLAYER] (user input), [GUIDE] (mentor movement), [PATH] (layout cues), [CONTROL] (avatar-to-vehicle transitions). Fail-safe ladder rungs use this same tag set.

Design Rationale

  • Science of Reading Alignment: targets RI.4.3 — cause-and-effect relationships and signal-word instruction — assuming baseline grade-level decoding mastery.
  • Embodied Mechanic Reinforcement: each mini-game's core mechanic enacts what was just read rather than quizzing it. Reinforcement is structural and kinesthetic.
  • Genuine Independent Application: YOU DO's final checkpoint tests a paragraph never modeled or scaffolded, so "no frame" means real transfer, not rehearsed recall.
  • Gradual Release (Fisher & Frey): scaffolding scales from expert think-aloud (I DO) to interactive text selection (WE DO) to open-text synthesis (YOU DO).
  • Vygotskian Scaffolding: checkpoint design fades structural supports — heavy → moderate → minimal — as competence is established.
  • Targeted Branching: diagnostic detours trigger on classic Grade 4 cause-effect errors, contextualized within the narrative to protect motivation.
  • Gamified Retrieval Practice: mini-games function as immediate retrieval practice loops following explicit instruction.
  • Repeatable/Scalable Architecture: pre-scripted misconception paths and immutable environmental transitions establish a production blueprint duplicable across any CCSS passage or standard.
